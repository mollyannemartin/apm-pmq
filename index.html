EXAM MODE

Exam strategy

Know the format

  • Closed-book online exam.
  • 2.5 hours, split into two parts with an optional break of up to 30 minutes.
  • 40 questions and 90 marks.
  • 20 multiple-response questions × 1 mark.
  • 5 select-from-list questions × 2 marks.
  • 5 short-response questions × 2 marks.
  • 10 long-response questions × 5 marks.

For written answers

  • Answer the exact command in the question.
  • For outline, give the requested points concisely.
  • For describe, give characteristics or features.
  • For explain, give the point and why/how it matters.
  • For differentiate, compare the items rather than describing one and then the other.
  • Use the scenario when one is provided. Generic textbook dumping wastes effort.

A sensible pacing starting point

  • Do not spend five minutes wrestling with a 1-mark question.
  • Use the higher-mark questions to demonstrate understanding clearly.
  • Leave time to check unanswered questions and improve weak responses.
  • Practise typing concise, structured answers because your real exam is online.

Your answer structure

Point → Explanation → Application.

For example: name the relevant concept, explain why it matters, then connect it directly to the scenario. For a long response, repeat this structure for each distinct point requested.

Command verbs to practise

StateGive the fact. OutlineGive the main features/points. DescribeGive characteristics or a clear account. ExplainSay what it is and why/how it matters. DifferentiateShow the meaningful differences. InterpretUse the information to reach a reasoned meaning/conclusion.

REVISION PLAN

How I would revise if I were you

Phase 1 — Before the course

Build familiarity. Work through the 24 topics. Do not try to memorise everything. Mark anything you cannot explain in your own words.

Phase 2 — During the course

Use this site alongside the workbook. Add corrections and examples from the instructor. If the course teaches a different/current definition, update this site.

Phase 3 — Between teaching days

Use active recall. Pick weak topics, hide the answers, and explain them aloud. Complete the homework/mock exam seriously.

Phase 4 — Final run-in

Stop endlessly rereading. Do timed questions. Focus on application, command verbs, distinctions and your weakest learning objectives.
