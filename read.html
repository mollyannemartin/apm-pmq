🎯 The 24-objective exam radar

These are not copied questions. They are the practical things the sample papers suggest you should be able to recognise, explain or apply for each objective.

1. Life cycles

Choose between linear, iterative, hybrid, evolutionary/incremental and extended approaches from the scenario; know why uncertainty and feedback point towards iterative approaches.

2. Governance arrangements

Know governance, policies, roles, delegated authority, tolerances and escalation. Expect questions about alignment with organisational objectives and decision rights.

3. Sustainability

Think beyond environmental impact: social, economic and governance/administrative considerations, monitoring and reporting throughout the project.

4. Business case

Know the main business-case areas and how strategic alignment, value, cost, risk and options support continued justification.

5. Procurement

Know procurement strategy and supplier selection, including cost, quality, delivery, risk and reimbursement/payment approaches.

6. Reviews

Know why projects are reviewed, what can trigger re-planning and how reviews support decisions and lessons learned.

7. Assurance

Distinguish assurance from day-to-day management and review. Know that assurance provides confidence to stakeholders/governance and can occur at multiple points.

8. Transition management

Know what must happen to move project outputs into use: knowledge transfer, readiness, adoption and avoiding poorly managed handover consequences.

9. Benefits management

Know the five-stage sequence: identification, definition, planning, tracking and realisation — and understand what each stage is for.

10. Stakeholder engagement & communication

Be able to use stakeholder analysis to shape communication, manage expectations and recognise the consequences of neglecting key stakeholders.

11. Conflict resolution

Know causes, positive/negative effects and approaches such as the Thomas-Kilmann modes. The sample papers test application, not just names.

12. Leadership

Expect scenario questions about adapting leadership style, coaching/mentoring, emotional intelligence, uncertainty and team needs.

13. Team management

Know team development, cohesion, collaboration, shared accountability and how hybrid/remote working can change leadership needs.

14. Diversity & inclusion

Understand why different perspectives can improve performance and what behaviours create an inclusive, positive environment.

15. Ethics, compliance & professionalism

Know professional conduct, CPD, legal/regulatory responsibilities and where to obtain specialist advice or standards.

16. Requirements management

Know how to define, prioritise and manage requirements. The 2026 sample paper repeatedly points towards value to the end user rather than simply choosing what is easiest.

17. Solutions development

Understand how solution development links to requirements, life cycle choice, feedback and delivering value.

18. Quality management

Know quality planning, quality plans, measurable quality criteria and quality control techniques such as inspection/testing.

19. Integrated planning

Understand how plans fit together and how risk, contingency, stakeholders and resources interact rather than being managed in isolated silos.

20. Schedule management

Know re-estimation, dependencies, critical path thinking, schedule reviews and actions that can recover a project that is behind schedule.

21. Resource management

Know resource requirements, capability, availability, constraints, allocation and RAM/RACI. Understand the difference between resource needs and personal preference.

22. Budgeting & cost control

Know cost categorisation, monitoring, reporting, commitments, actuals/forecast and how financial information supports decisions.

23. Risk & issue management

Know the distinction between risk and issue and the process: identify, analyse, monitor, escalate, respond and close. Know when tolerances trigger escalation.

24. Change control

Know the controlled sequence from request through evaluation and recommendation to updating plans and implementation; detailed evaluation considers impacts before a decision.

What is different about the 2026 sample paper?
It is heavily scenario-based.

You are often given a project situation and asked to select, describe or explain the best response. Knowing a definition is not enough; you need to recognise when it applies.

It mixes tiny decisions with 5-mark explanations.

A 1-mark question may ask you to identify the best option, while a 5-mark question expects several distinct points. Your revision therefore needs both fast recognition and structured written answers.

It tests distinctions.

Examples include assurance vs management, risk vs issue, life-cycle choice, change-control stages, and what belongs in planning or transition.

It keeps returning to application.

Value, stakeholder needs, uncertainty, governance, risk, quality, resources and change are repeatedly placed into realistic project situations.

EXAM MODE

Exam strategy

Know the format

  • Closed-book online exam.
  • 2.5 hours, split into two parts with an optional break of up to 30 minutes.
  • 40 questions and 90 marks.
  • 20 multiple-response questions × 1 mark.
  • 5 select-from-list questions × 2 marks.
  • 5 short-response questions × 2 marks.
  • 10 long-response questions × 5 marks.

For written answers

  • Answer the exact command in the question.
  • For outline, give the requested points concisely.
  • For describe, give characteristics or features.
  • For explain, give the point and why/how it matters.
  • For differentiate, compare the items rather than describing one and then the other.
  • Use the scenario when one is provided. Generic textbook dumping wastes effort.

A sensible pacing starting point

  • Do not spend five minutes wrestling with a 1-mark question.
  • Use the higher-mark questions to demonstrate understanding clearly.
  • Leave time to check unanswered questions and improve weak responses.
  • Practise typing concise, structured answers because your real exam is online.

Your answer structure

Point → Explanation → Application.

For example: name the relevant concept, explain why it matters, then connect it directly to the scenario. For a long response, repeat this structure for each distinct point requested.

Command verbs to practise

StateGive the fact. OutlineGive the main features/points. DescribeGive characteristics or a clear account. ExplainSay what it is and why/how it matters. DifferentiateShow the meaningful differences. InterpretUse the information to reach a reasoned meaning/conclusion.

REVISION PLAN

How I would revise if I were you

Phase 1 — Before the course

Build familiarity. Work through the 24 topics. Do not try to memorise everything. Mark anything you cannot explain in your own words.

Phase 2 — During the course

Use this site alongside the workbook. Add corrections and examples from the instructor. If the course teaches a different/current definition, update this site.

Phase 3 — Between teaching days

Use active recall. Pick weak topics, hide the answers, and explain them aloud. Complete the homework/mock exam seriously.

Phase 4 — Final run-in

Stop endlessly rereading. Do timed questions. Focus on application, command verbs, distinctions and your weakest learning objectives.
